# USER_INTERVIEWS.md

Three conversations conducted during the build week. Names used with permission; company details lightly anonymized at interviewees' request.

## Interview 1 — Rohan K., CTO, 11-person B2B SaaS (Seed)

**Date:** Day 2 of build week | **Duration:** 14 minutes | **Channel:** Twitter DM → video call

**Background:** Rohan runs engineering at a fintech tool for SMB accountants. Team of 6 engineers, 3 PMs, 2 designers. Approved all AI tool subscriptions personally.

**Direct quotes:**

> "I look at the bill every month and I know it's too high. But fixing it feels like a $200 problem that requires $500 of my time to solve."

> "We're on Cursor Business for 6 people and ChatGPT Team for 8. I literally could not tell you why we're on Business vs Pro for Cursor — someone just said 'get the good one.'"

> "If you showed me a page that said 'here's the exact seat waste and here's how to fix it,' I'd act on that in 30 minutes. The information is the problem, not the motivation."

**Most surprising thing:** He didn't know Cursor Business added SSO/admin controls — he thought it was just "more compute." He'd been paying an extra $20/seat/month for 6 months for a feature he didn't know existed and didn't need.

**What it changed:** Added the 1-sentence "why this plan matters" to each recommendation card. The savings number isn't enough — users need to understand what they're giving up when they downgrade, even if the answer is "nothing relevant to you."

## Interview 2 — Priya M., Engineering Manager, 28-person Series A

**Date:** Day 4 | **Duration:** 11 minutes | **Channel:** LinkedIn message → call

**Background:** EM at a developer tools company. Manages a team of 14 engineers. AI budget is technically under her, but purchases go through finance approval above $500/mo.

**Direct quotes:**

> "I don't care about the $50/month stuff. I care about the stuff where I can go to my CFO and say 'I found a real line item to cut.'"

> "If this thing surfaces something under $100, I'm not going to act on it. If it surfaces $400+, I'll use the report as the artifact to get the approval to switch."

> "The thing I'd really want is a benchmark — like, are we normal? Is $120/engineer/month on AI tools high or low for a company at our stage?"

**Most surprising thing:** She assumed every team her size had already optimized their AI spend. She was surprised that "just auditing it" wasn't already standard practice. "I thought finance teams were doing this. Turns out nobody is."

**What it changed:** Added the tier-based framing (>$500/mo = high, surfaces Credex prominently). Low-savings results now explicitly acknowledge they're doing well — Priya's mental model was binary ("either I'm wasting a lot or I'm fine"), so the honest "you're spending well" verdict is itself valuable. Also added the benchmark feature to the bonus list.

## Interview 3 — Marcus T., Solo Technical Founder, pre-revenue

**Date:** Day 5 | **Duration:** 18 minutes | **Channel:** Indie Hackers Slack → video call

**Background:** Building a no-code internal tooling product alone. 1 person, using Claude Pro + Cursor Pro + ChatGPT Plus. ~$60/month total. Very cost-conscious.

**Direct quotes:**

> "I know I'm not the big customer here. I'm spending $60/month. But I check Hacker News every morning and I would 100% use a tool like this just to validate that I'm not missing something obvious."

> "What I actually want to know is: given that I do coding AND writing, is there a single tool that covers most of both? Because right now I feel like I'm paying for overlap."

> "I shared an Anthropic invoice in a Discord once to ask if I was spending too much and got like 8 different answers. Nobody actually knows what 'normal' is."

**Most surprising thing:** He had Claude Pro AND ChatGPT Plus running simultaneously, using them for different tasks by feel ("Claude for thinking, ChatGPT for quick stuff"). He'd never compared their actual capabilities systematically. He was genuinely uncertain if he was wasting $20/month on the second one. The audit correctly flagged this as low-savings but the reasoning — "you might be paying for capability overlap" — was the thing he most wanted to see explicitly stated.

**What it changed:** Added the use-case dimension to the tool evaluation more prominently. The "primary use case" field in the form now more directly influences the recommendations (e.g., if use case is "mixed," we're less aggressive about switching tool recommendations since the user may genuinely need both). Also confirmed that solo founders ARE a valid early adopter segment even at low spend — they're high-shareability users who post on HN and Discord.
